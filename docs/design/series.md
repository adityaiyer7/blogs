# Blog series

**Status:** active
**Last updated:** 2026-08-04
**PRs:** #47

Implements [#35](https://github.com/adityaiyer7/blogs/issues/35). Author-facing
instructions live in [`README.md`](../../README.md#series); this document covers the
decisions, the evidence behind them, and what a future change should be careful not to
undo. Closely related: [`docs/document_pins.md`](../document_pins.md), whose D5 is the
precedent this builds on.

## Problem

`categories` is a broad subject taxonomy — Mathematics, Interpretability, Personal. It
cannot also express an intentionally ordered reading sequence. An SVD → CCA → CKA
progression would be three Mathematics posts scattered among every other Mathematics
post, ordered by publication date rather than by concept.

Publication order is the second half of the problem. CKA might be written before CCA,
and unrelated posts keep publishing while a series is half finished, so a series cannot
be modelled as "these consecutive posts" either.

## Decision

Three optional, flat front-matter fields, treated as an all-or-nothing set:

```yaml
series-id: representational-geometry                          # stable, URL-safe, used for filtering and the URL
series: "Representational Geometry from First Principles"     # reader-facing title
series-order: 10                                              # conceptual position, unrelated to date
```

Conceptual order uses gaps (10, 20, 30) so a prerequisite can be slotted in later
without renumbering, the same convention as `pin-order`.

Three surfaces consume them:

| Surface | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Series landing page | A normal Quarto page with a listing: `include: series-id`, `sort: ["series-order asc"]` |
| Homepage listing cards | A linked label injected by `blogposts/scripts/inject_listing_badges.py` |
| Post pages | A banner generated by `blogposts/filters/series_banner.lua` |

Series ordering applies **only** on the landing page. The five homepage listings keep
`["pinned desc", "pin-order asc", "date desc"]` untouched — on the homepage, series
membership is something a card *displays*, never something that moves it.

### D1 — No defaults file was needed, unlike pinning

**Decision.** No `_series_defaults.yml`. Nothing was added to `metadata-files:`.

**Why this was in question.** Pinning needed `_pin_defaults.yml` because a multi-field
sort has nothing to compare when posts omit the fields, and D2 there leans on a `999999`
sentinel. Series looked like the same shape of problem.

**Evidence.** Probed against Quarto 1.9.36 before anything was built, the same way D1 of
`document_pins.md` was. Three posts were temporarily tagged (orders 10, 20, 100) and a
throwaway landing page rendered:

- `include: {series-id: …}` matched exactly those three. The two untagged posts were
  excluded — a listing `include:` on a field a document simply does not have excludes
  it, so no project-wide default is required to make filtering work.
- `sort: ["series-order asc"]` produced 10 → 20 → 100, which is numeric, not
  lexicographic (`"100" < "20"` as strings would have inverted it), and which inverted
  the posts' publication order, confirming the two axes are independent.
- With `series-order` deleted from one tagged post, that post sorted **last** rather
  than breaking the sort — natively the same outcome the pinning sentinel is built to
  produce. A sentinel default would have bought nothing.

**Consequence.** A `series-id` default must never be added project-wide: it would put
every post in that series. A `series-order` default is merely redundant.

### D2 — `contents:` must be an explicit glob, not a directory

The obvious spelling, `contents: ../../posts`, renders a landing page with **zero
cards and no warning**. So does a project-root-relative `contents: /posts`. Only
`contents: ../../posts/*/index.qmd` resolves from a page nested two levels down.

This is recorded because the failure is silent and looks exactly like "the filter
matched nothing". Anyone debugging an empty series page should check this first.

### D3 — The homepage label shares the pin badge's injector rather than adding a second one

**Decision.** `blogposts/scripts/inject_pin_badges.py` was renamed to
`inject_listing_badges.py` and generalized: one front-matter scan, one `CARD_RE`, one
markup-drift guard, one pass, emitting both the pinned badge and the series label.

**Why not a custom listing field or an EJS template.** Already settled in
`document_pins.md` D5 and not re-litigated: Quarto's built-in listing renders a fixed
field set and silently ignores custom ones, and a custom template replaces the entire
card — including the base64 `data-categories` payload the category filter depends on —
against a Quarto version CI does not pin.

**Why not a second script.** D5 anticipated this moment and warned against two
mechanisms for the same cards. A second script would mean two copies of the coupling to
Quarto's card markup, and a Quarto upgrade would silently fix one and break the other.
D5's stated trigger — "if a custom template gets built for series labels anyway, the
badge should move into it" — did not fire, because no template was built.

**Cost accepted.** The rename loses `git blame` continuity on that file, and its test
file moved with it (`tests/test_pin_badges.py` → `tests/test_listing_badges.py`).

### D4 — The label's link is derived from the card's own href

The injector does not know the site root, and must not: the site is served from the
`/blogs/` subpath on GitHub Pages, and the same markup is written for pages at different
depths. Each card already contains a link of the form `<prefix>posts/<slug>/index.html`,
so the label reuses `<prefix>` and emits `<prefix>series/<series-id>/index.html`. Verified
correct as `./series/…` on the homepage.

### D5 — The label is suppressed on series landing pages

Every card on `/series/<id>/` belongs to that series, so labelling each one repeats the
page title once per row. `is_series_page()` gates it.

The **pinned badge is deliberately left visible there**. This is the one judgement call
in this change worth revisiting: a "Pinned" badge on a page that sorts by
`series-order` advertises a sort the page does not apply. It was kept on the grounds
that "Pinned" states a fact about the post rather than about the current sort, and
because leaving pin behaviour untouched was the narrower change. Flipping it is a
one-argument change if it reads as noise in practice.

### D6 — The banner is a Lua filter, and its position depends on filter order

`filters/series_banner.lua` builds the banner from front matter and inserts it at block
1, modelled on `filters/reading-time.lua`. It must not be written into post bodies:
`sync_post.sh` regenerates bodies from Obsidian while preserving repository-managed
front matter, so a hand-written banner would be destroyed on the next sync.

**The ordering is load-bearing and invisible.** Both filters insert at block 1, so the
one registered *later* in `_quarto.yml` ends up on top. `series_banner.lua` is
registered after `reading-time.lua` for exactly this reason. `styles.css` depends on the
same fact: `.reading-time`'s negative top margin is neutralized by a
`.series-banner + .reading-time` rule. Reordering the filters silently changes the
layout.

The banner's link is site-root-relative (`/series/<id>/`). Quarto rewrites those at
render time — verified: it emitted `../../series/representational-geometry/` from a post
page, which is correct under the `/blogs/` subpath.

### D7 — No "part N of M"

The banner names the series and links to it. It does not say "Part 2 of 5".

`series-order` values are sparse by convention, a series is explicitly allowed to be
published incomplete and out of order, and a post whose `kind` is disabled disappears
from listings entirely (D8). Any numerator or denominator computed at render time would
therefore be wrong as soon as the series grew or a kind was toggled. The landing page
shows the real sequence, which is always current.

### D8 — A draft-hidden series post silently leaves its landing page

When a `kind` is disabled in `_post_visibility.yml`,
`scripts/update_post_visibility.py` writes those posts into `website.drafts`, and
Quarto drops drafts from every listing.

**Decided behaviour: the post disappears from its series landing page, the remaining
posts keep their relative order, and no gap, placeholder, or "coming soon" row is
shown.** Verified: with `mini-research` disabled, the tagged mini-research post vanished
from the series page while the other two stayed in conceptual order, and the sitemap
dropped it too.

This is the right default because it is the same rule everywhere else on the site —
visibility is enforced upstream of every listing, and series pages get no exemption.
It does mean a series page can look like it is missing a step. That is the same
situation as a series that simply has not been finished yet, which the landing page also
cannot distinguish, and #35 explicitly rules out placeholder documents for unwritten
entries.

### D9 — One linter rule, not four

`qmd_lint` rule **F5** (WARNING) fires on partial series metadata, a `series-id` that is
not lowercase kebab-case, and a `series-order` that is not an integer.

The failure it exists for is genuinely silent: a post with `series:` but no `series-id:`
renders perfectly, is simply absent from its landing page, and nothing anywhere reports
why. A `series-order` that YAML read as a string (`"10"`) sorts lexicographically
against the integers.

**Cross-document checks were rejected**, including the duplicate-`series-order` check
#35 floated. `collect_findings` in `tools/qmd_lint/engine.py` runs rules over a single
parsed `Document`; there is no cross-document phase, and adding one to support a warning
about two posts sharing an order value — which produces an arbitrary but stable order,
not a broken page — is not a trade worth making.

### D10 — Shipped with nothing in a series

No post carries series metadata on merge, and no landing page exists yet. This follows
pinning's D6: the mechanism is live, and creating a series is an editorial act. Every
behaviour above was verified with temporary probe metadata that was reverted before
commit.

`blogposts/series/_metadata.yml` is the one committed artifact — shared settings that a
future landing page inherits. A directory containing only that file renders no page.

## Behavior and contract

- All three fields are optional. A post with none of them is unaffected everywhere:
  verified byte-identical homepage markup apart from the injected labels.
- The three fields are all-or-nothing. A partial set draws an F5 warning, is skipped by
  the injector (no label), and is skipped by the banner filter.
- Series ordering applies on landing pages only. Homepage sort is unchanged.
- A series may be incomplete and published out of conceptual order. Nothing fails.
- Adding a published post to an existing series requires only the three fields; the
  landing page updates on the next render.
- RSS is unaffected. `document_pins.md` D4 established that Quarto builds feeds in date
  order regardless of a listing's display sort, and the feed does not currently generate
  at all (`website.title` is unset). Confirmed unchanged, not rebuilt.

## Known gaps and follow-ups

- **No previous/next navigation within a series.** #35 permits deferring it. It needs
  each post to know its neighbours, which no current mechanism provides — the banner
  filter sees one document at a time, and the injector runs after render. It would need
  either a generated registry or a second post-render pass.
- **No `/series/` index of all series.** Landing pages are reachable from post banners
  and homepage labels only, until one is written by hand.
- **`series` is repeated in every post of a series** and can drift between them. A
  registry would fix it; #35 rules one out until richer navigation justifies it. The
  landing page title is the authority a reader sees on the series page itself.
- **The injector tracks Quarto's card markup.** Guarded by the loud failure inherited
  from D5, but a Quarto upgrade that restructures listing cards needs `CARD_RE` updated —
  now in one place instead of two.
- **`create_post.sh` does not prompt for series metadata.** Left out to keep the change
  minimal. Unlike pinning (D7 there), series membership is often known at creation time,
  so this is a reasonable future addition rather than a settled "no".
